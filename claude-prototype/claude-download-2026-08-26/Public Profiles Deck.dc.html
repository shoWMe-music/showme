01 — The problem

One URL. Two audiences that want opposite things.

Today's public profile is the private profile with the edit buttons hidden — a cover, a bio, a list. It converts nobody, and it publishes riders to the open web.

The fan
“When are they playing?”

Next show, tickets, a video, a way to follow. Nothing else.

The booker
“Are they free, and are they real?”

Open dates, party size, rider, and proof the money side works.

The risk
Serve both badly, or leak.

Riders and stage plots must never be fetchable by the open web.

02 — The move

Two lanes, one page.

The page opens as a fan page and ends as a booking page. Nothing is hidden from the audience — the industry material simply sits below, in its own visual register, behind its own gate.

Live availability and ticket state come from the event data — the page is never stale.
Trust signals only shoWMe can issue: verified payout, rider on file, events settled.
Documents are signed-in only, and every download is logged back to the owner.

03 — Performer

The next show is the headline.

Tickets is the hero action. Doors, support and remaining stock are pulled from the event, so the page tells a fan everything they need without a click.

04 — Venue

The programme is the page.
