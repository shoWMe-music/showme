### [MAJOR] Settlement — a deal's named payer pays nothing; the operators' residual absorbs it, and the Budget Planner never shows it

**As:** `operator@` on the Album Release (`e2e00000-0000-4000-8000-0000000000e1`).

**Steps**
1. Create a deal on the event naming **Neon Tide as `payer`** and **Priya Sound as `payee`**,
   structure `guarantee`, SEK 2,500. (The composer offers every participant and does not require
   the operator to be one of them.)
2. Have both parties confirm it.
3. `POST /events/…e1/settlement/compute`.
4. Cancel the deal and compute again — the control.

**Expected.** Neon Tide agreed to pay SEK 2,500 and should bear it. decisions **§25.7.1**'s stated
grounds are general and in one line: *"a party cannot be charged for an agreement it is not a party
to. `role_in_deal` already records who owes it, so the engine had the fact and was not reading it."*
The two operators are not parties to this agreement.

**Actual.** The engine credits the payee and lets the **pool** fund it, so the shortfall lands on
the residual and is divided by the operators alone:

```
with the deal live (cost split 70/30):
  b1 The Lantern Hall    entitlement -175000   residual -175000  rbp 7000
  b3 Neon Tide (PAYER)   entitlement 2000000   residual       0     ← unchanged
  b4 Priya Sound         entitlement  250000
  bb Northlight          entitlement  -75000   residual  -75000  rbp 3000
  transfers: b1→b4 175000, bb→b4 75000          Σ net = 0

after cancelling the same deal (control):
  b1 entitlement 0   b3 entitlement 2000000   b4 entitlement 0   bb entitlement 0
  transfers: b1→b2 3000000, b1→b3 2000000      Σ net = 0
```

The delta is exactly −250,000 split 70/30 between the two operators; Neon Tide's figure does not
move by a minor unit either way. The route *does* resolve the payer —
`routes/settlement.ts:944` takes `parties.find(p => p.roleInDeal === "payer")` — but
`packages/settlement/src/reconcile.ts` reads `payerParticipantId` only in the **rental** pass
(line 291) and the **advance/prepaid** pass (line 444). A `performance` / `guarantee` deal has no
branch that charges its payer.

**Second half, on the planner.** The same SEK 2,500 is invisible in the Budget Planner.
`useBudgetSeed.ts::performerFeeOf` filters entitled parties to `performers.has(participantId)`; a
crew payee gives `onTheBill.length === 0` and the function returns `null`. So the planner reads
`TOTAL COSTS SEK 84,245` and `PROFIT / LOSS −SEK 1,245` for a night the settlement then charges the
operators an extra SEK 2,500 for, and the two screens disagree by exactly that amount.

**Evidence:** the two compute responses above, taken four minutes apart on the same event with
nothing else changed; `docs/screenshots/qa-2026-10-01-run14/signatures-survive-a-recompute.png`
shows the operator's card reading *"Your 50% of what is left after every other party is paid —
−SEK 1,250"* at the 50/50 stage of the same probe.

**Scope.** Engine-level, so every account kind and every night. Not checked: a `split` deal between
two non-operator parties, and whether an `advance` on such a deal behaves differently (the prepaid
pass *does* read the payer).

   The MCP `fill` tool **appends** to this controlled `Input` rather than replacing: `50` + `70` =
   `5070`, which `Math.min(100, …)` clamps to 100. A second `fill` of `70` into the now-`100` field
   changed nothing at all, which is what gave it away. Driving the field with the native value setter
   plus an `input` event produced `70 / 30` and `{b1: 7000, bb: 3000}` first time. **Reading only the
   database would have produced a wrong MAJOR about the co-promotion split.**
2. **"Cancelling the first hold does not promote the second."** True, and for two different reasons
   at once — which is why the first version of this finding was wrong. The route I used
   (`PATCH status`) genuinely does not promote (filed as a MINOR), **but** the hold I was watching
   also had `hold_auto_promote = false`, so even the correct `/hold/release` route would have left it
   alone. Only running the control — turning auto-promote on and releasing again, which moved it 2 →
   1 — separated the two, and the second half became its own MINOR.
3. **"The ticket-tier estimate saved as 0100."** It did, literally, for the same append reason as
   (1) — and it parsed to 100 and stored `{"est": 100}`. A finding about a corrupted tier would have
   been my typing, not the app. (Per the brief: a fumbled input that produces a bare 500 is worth
   chasing; this one produced the right number and was not.)
4. **"The share document route is broken — 401 with a valid token."** Three schemes in a row were
   refused: a cookie, `x-share-token`, and `Authorization: Bearer`. The route wants
   `Authorization: **ShareBearer** <jwt>` (`routes/shares.ts:218`), deliberately distinct from the
   Firebase bearer parsed twelve lines below it. With the right scheme it answers 200. A finding
   filed after any of the first three would have been about my request.

And one near-finding recorded so the next sweep does not chase it: **the operator gets no
notification when a party signs a deal the operator authored but is not a party to.**
`dealRecipients` is deal parties plus delegated agents, by design (*"a performer must not learn that
another party's terms moved"*), and the author is neither. It is the reason the Deals tab read
*"0 of 2 signed"* while the 409 it had just produced said one party had signed. I have **not** filed
it separately because it is the same missing-publish mechanism as the cancel MAJOR and would be
fixed by the same change; noting it so the fix is scoped to cover the author.
